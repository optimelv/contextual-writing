# Commercial content

## Commercial context

Start with the customer, user, partner, or buyer. Connect the offer to a real workflow, problem, priority, or decision. Establish the relevant stage: discovery, first outreach, active evaluation, proposal, follow-up, adoption, renewal, or general marketing.

## Outreach

Research the known recipient or company before using personalization. Separate sourced facts from fit hypotheses. A good message states a relevant reason for contact, credible value, and one proportionate next step. Do not invent familiarity, trigger events, buyer pain, reputation, contacts, or urgency.

For sales email and message details, read [sales outreach](sales-outreach.md). Do not turn a public trigger into a private customer need: a hiring spike, funding event, launch, or expansion can justify contact, but the resulting priority, pain, cost, and buying intent remain hypotheses unless grounded in customer or account evidence.

This workflow owns outreach that sells an offer to a customer, buyer, or partner. Applicant-to-employer, founder, investor, accelerator, or hiring-team outreach seeking a role or internship belongs to career documents, even when it uses similar persuasion principles.

## Follow-up

Use grounded call or thread evidence. Preserve what was agreed, who owns what, and when. Label proposed actions as proposed. Keep internal commentary out of external copy.

## Value and proof

Translate capabilities into the audience's decision criteria. Use proof that actually supports the claim. State assumptions and ranges. If credible math is not possible, provide a structural value case and the smallest validation plan instead of fabricated ROI.

Keep the claim ladder explicit. Access does not establish trust, a possible buyer or partner does not establish recurring revenue, workflow presence does not establish switching costs, and process integration does not by itself establish defensibility. When evidence stops, convert the next claim into a hypothesis, validation question, or proposed test.

## Competitive content

Compare official claims, confirmed facts, independent evidence, and field signals separately. State where evidence is mixed or missing. Include what not to overclaim. Do not invent competitor capabilities, pricing, customers, account presence, or weaknesses.

## Marketing and web copy

Clarify audience, awareness stage, traffic source, offer, objection, proof, brand voice, and call to action. Use persuasive language when supported. Avoid generic superlatives, fake scarcity, vague authority, and unsupported transformation claims. A landing page, thought-leadership post, product page, and investor narrative require different evidence and structure.
