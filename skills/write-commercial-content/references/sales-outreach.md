# Sales outreach

Use this reference for customer-, buyer-, or partner-facing sales email and message drafts. Applicant-to-employer outreach remains with career documents.

## Select the motion before the structure

Distinguish:

- **Personalized cold first touch:** the recipient has not engaged; relevance must be earned without implying a relationship.
- **Warm or referred introduction:** the shared context or authorized referral can lead; a public trigger is optional.
- **Active opportunity or post-call follow-up:** use verified conversation, CRM, or thread evidence and preserve commitments.
- **Broad campaign:** personalization depth, testing, compliance, and deliverability constraints differ from one-to-one outreach.

Do not force the same subject, length, meeting ask, proof line, or follow-up cadence across these motions.

## Research-to-message ledger

For a personalized cold first touch, resolve the smallest grounded chain that supports the message:

1. **Relevance trigger:** a concrete, attributable reason for contact, with source and date when volatile.
2. **Implication hypothesis:** what the trigger may change for this audience.
3. **Problem hypothesis:** one workflow, priority, or friction that may be relevant.
4. **Consequence hypothesis:** the operational or commercial effect, only at the strength the evidence permits.
5. **Proof:** one truthful, attributable result or comparable example that supports the proposed relevance.
6. **Next step:** one easy-to-answer action appropriate to the relationship and stage.

The trigger can be verified while every downstream implication remains uncertain. Never convert the chain into customer fact merely because it makes the email flow. Use language such as `may`, `often`, `teams in this situation`, or a direct question where uncertainty is real.

If no sourceable trigger or account-native reason exists, do not invent personalization. Either research the gap, ask for the missing anchor, or draft a transparent role- or problem-led message that does not pretend to know the recipient's situation.

## Drafting principles

- Put the recipient's context or problem hypothesis before a feature list.
- Keep one central problem or outcome per short first-touch message.
- Use the buyer's working language; remove internal category labels, sales jargon, feature dumps, and generic compliments.
- Include proof only when it is accurate, attributable, and relevant enough to support the attached claim. Keep metric scope, customer type, and conditions visible where material.
- Ask one proportionate next step. For an early cold touch, an interest or relevance question can be lower friction than immediately requesting a meeting. For warm, referred, formal, or active motions, a meeting or concrete action can be appropriate.
- Make the subject recognizable, specific, and non-deceptive. Put useful information before curiosity tricks.
- Keep the draft concise enough to scan in its real channel, but follow a supplied hard limit or tested house convention rather than a universal word target.

One useful optional scaffold is:

`verified trigger -> tentative implication -> one problem or consequence -> relevant proof or solution -> one low-friction action`

Omit any element that lacks evidence or adds no value. Do not fill a framework by inventing a pain, causal chain, social proof, or urgency.

## Follow-ups

A follow-up should add a useful new reason, proof point, question, stakeholder angle, or clarification. Do not send an empty `just bumping this` sequence by default. Stop or change course when the recipient declines, opts out, the claim is no longer current, or applicable law and organizational policy require it.

Cadence, number of touches, subject length, word count, reading level, ROI timing, and meeting versus interest CTA are context-dependent hypotheses. They vary by market, channel, audience, offer, sender reputation, legal basis, and tested performance. Do not present a practitioner or vendor threshold as a universal rule.

## Safety and final review

- Keep the message draft-only unless the user separately authorizes sending or external draft creation.
- Respect applicable consent, identification, opt-out, privacy, and anti-spam requirements; use legal research when jurisdiction controls the message.
- Exclude internal account strategy, opportunity probability, margins, negotiation posture, private notes, and unsupported competitor claims.
- Apply the shared submission-readiness gate. Verify one semantic action, recipient-only content, source-to-claim fit, and a name-swap test on target-specific lines.
- Treat punctuation as house style. A dash is not an authorship verdict, and German permits the Gedankenstrich; change punctuation only for clarity or an actual style requirement.
