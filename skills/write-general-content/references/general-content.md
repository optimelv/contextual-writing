# General content

## Genre selection

An article explains or argues. A blog post may foreground author perspective. A newsletter depends on an established reader relationship. A social post requires channel-native compression. Website prose helps a visitor understand or act. Personal writing may preserve ambiguity, humor, fragments, and uneven rhythm.

Do not use one structure across all genres. Establish what the reader already knows, what should change after reading, and what evidence or experience supports the piece.

## Voice

Use the author's samples and stated preferences as a soft guide. Do not invent opinions, anecdotes, reactions, humor, or lived experience. Neutral prose is correct when the author has not supplied a personal stance or the genre requires neutrality.

## Structure

Open with the substance, scene, question, or claim that fits the genre. Give each paragraph a useful job. Use headings only when they improve navigation. Vary length where it serves meaning, not to simulate humanity.

## Evidence and persuasion

Source external claims according to the risk and genre. Do not disguise uncertainty through fluent prose. In opinion writing, distinguish personal judgment from factual support. In web and social content, preserve the difference between proof, illustration, and marketing claim.

## Editing

Remove filler, generic praise, vague attribution, repeated conclusions, and formulaic rhetoric only when they weaken this text. Preserve deliberate repetition, rhythm, punctuation, and informal language when supported by context.
