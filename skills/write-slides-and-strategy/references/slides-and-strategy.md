# Slides and strategy

## General narrative

- define the audience decision or presentation objective
- use a governing question and answer
- make slide titles assert conclusions when the evidence supports them
- keep one main message per slide and make the body prove it
- match density to time, audience, and speaker-led or sendable mode
- use evidence that changes interpretation or action
- move detailed support, methods, and sources to notes or appendix when appropriate
- use charts, tables, and diagrams to explain relationships, not decorate
- preserve supplied templates, brand systems, and slide masters

## Consulting and strategy

Structure around the decision, decisive evidence, implication, recommendation, risk, and next action. Separate case facts from assumptions. Show units, time basis, and calculation logic. Do not force a framework that does not answer the case.

## Startup and investor

Clarify problem, user or buyer, current alternative, product, proof, business model, distribution, market scope, economics, defensibility mechanism, team relevance, ask, and use of funds only where evidence exists. Match the stage. An idea-stage deck should not imitate traction.

Distinguish opportunity quality from validation confidence. Foreign analogues are comparisons, not proof of local demand. Use open fields for missing internal metrics.

Apply an explicit claim ladder to commercial narratives:

- access or a referral channel is not proof of trust, conversion, or repeat use;
- an addressable payer, buyer, insurer, or partner is not proof of willingness to pay or recurring revenue;
- presence in a workflow is not proof of switching cost or durable defensibility;
- an operational process is not difficult to replicate merely because it takes time;
- a comparison such as asset ownership versus integration belongs only when both are real strategic alternatives in the case.

When the evidence does not support the next step in the ladder, state a hypothesis or validation need instead of a moat, scale, trust, or revenue conclusion. An uncertainty note outside the slide or script does not cure overconfident recipient-visible copy.

Treat recurring revenue as a distinct claim, not a synonym for general revenue or a possible partnership. It requires evidence or a testable mechanism for repeat payment, renewal, usage, or contract recurrence. When a brief asks whether recurring revenue is established, address that category explicitly rather than substituting the broader phrase `revenue model`.

## Customer and sales presentations

Start with the customer's priorities, workflow, requirements, and decision. Introduce the seller only as the response. Exclude internal strategy, private account notes, opportunity probability, margins, negotiation posture, and unverified competitive claims. Label agreed, proposed, open, assumed, and unverified.

## Spoken scripts

Write for natural delivery, not document prose. Avoid adjacent sentences that repeat the same commercial fact. Preserve intentional cuts. Time the script against the real speaking limit.
