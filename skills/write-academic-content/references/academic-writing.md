# Academic writing

## Scope and evidence posture

Academic writing turns a defined question and supplied evidence into a defensible scholarly deliverable. It does not silently become a source-discovery task. Use the supplied brief, rubric, notes, data, extracts, and references first. If a necessary proposition lacks support, identify the gap or request the material that would resolve it.

Preserve the difference between what a source reports, what the evidence permits the author to infer, and what the text proposes. Retain uncertainty markers when evidence is limited, contested, observational, or context-bound. Do not upgrade association to causation, a finding to a universal claim, or a source's interpretation to settled fact.

## Deliverable modes

- **Paragraph:** Give the paragraph one analytical job. Connect claim, support, interpretation, and transition without mechanically forcing every element into every paragraph.
- **Abstract:** State the problem or objective, approach, central result or argument, and contribution only to the extent supported by the paper. Do not promise findings that the body does not establish.
- **Introduction:** Define the problem, its relevance, the focused gap or question, and the paper's purpose. Do not manufacture a research gap from broad claims of novelty.
- **Literature synthesis:** Compare positions, methods, contexts, and limits across sources. Organize by the analytical question, not by one-summary-per-author. When sources report conflicting findings about the same construct, contrast those findings in the same analytical block before introducing a source that addresses a different mechanism or boundary condition. Preserve disagreement and boundary conditions. A synthesis sentence at the end is not enough if the body still proceeds as isolated author capsules.
- **Method:** Describe only methods, sampling, materials, analytical steps, ethics, and limitations actually used or explicitly planned. Clearly distinguish a proposal from completed work.
- **Results:** Report observations and analyses without importing interpretation that belongs in discussion. Keep units, denominators, effect direction, and uncertainty intact.
- **Discussion:** Interpret results against the stated question and literature. Separate plausible explanation from demonstrated mechanism; state transfer limits and alternative explanations where material.
- **Conclusion:** Answer the question at the supported level of certainty. Summarize contribution, limits, and justified implications without adding new evidence.
- **Revision:** Preserve the original's claims, citations, and discipline conventions unless the user authorizes substantive changes. Explain material evidentiary gaps rather than concealing them with fluent wording.

## Citations, quotations, and attribution

Keep the requested citation style and supplied bibliographic identifiers unchanged. Do not fabricate a citation to make a paragraph look academic. A citation must remain attached to the proposition it supports; do not move it across materially different claims. Keep direct quotations exact, retain page references when supplied, and distinguish quotation from paraphrase. Treat technical categories as claim anchors: for example, `venture-backed` must not become general debt financing or another funding category merely because the output language changes.

Attribute positions where authorship matters: for example, use formulations equivalent to “X argues”, “the study reports”, or “the data indicate” when the evidence does not warrant a stronger statement. Do not substitute vague collective attribution such as “research shows” for a specific supplied source.

## German and English conventions

Match the target discipline, institution, and requested register before applying language preferences. In both languages, prefer precise verbs and explicit logical relations over decorative abstraction. German academic prose may use nominal forms where the discipline expects them, but avoid unnecessary nesting. English academic prose should not imitate German sentence architecture. Do not translate technical terms or citation conventions mechanically when the field uses an established English or German term.

Generic importance language is not analysis. Terms equivalent to `central`, `crucial`, `modern`, `future-oriented`, or `increasingly important` require a defined relationship or evidence; remove them when they only restate that the topic matters. A conclusion should state the bounded implication of the evidence, not repeat the opening proposition with a stronger adjective.

## Final review

Check the requested section's function, argument sequence, terminology, citation placement, quotation fidelity, claim strength, limitations, and format. Complete a final sentence-level pass for subject-verb agreement, grammatical case, pronoun and modifier attachment, spelling, punctuation, and accidental language mixing. Re-read every author-led reporting clause and verify singular or plural agreement from the written subject, not from the number of studies being discussed. Keep unresolved evidence needs visible. A polished passage with an unsupported empirical claim is not a finished academic output; an evidence-calibrated passage with avoidable grammar errors is not finished either.
