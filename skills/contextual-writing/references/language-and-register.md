# Language and register

## Selection

Follow the requested language. When none is specified, inherit the user's language or the existing document. Match regional, institutional, and organizational conventions when they matter.

English:

- choose British, American, or another convention from audience, destination, or source document
- keep vocabulary natural for the user's demonstrated proficiency and target audience
- do not imitate native-speaker slang merely to sound human
- use contractions only when the channel and relationship support them
- keep spelling, dates, punctuation, quotation style, and number conventions consistent with the selected English locale

German:

- select `du` or `Sie` from the relationship and keep it consistent
- prefer direct modern German in ordinary professional contexts
- preserve established technical, legal, academic, and institutional terminology
- avoid literal English syntax and artificial translations of established business terms
- keep `du` or `Sie`, salutations, closings, dates, decimal separators, and capitalization consistent with the destination convention

## Register gates

Neutral: explain or inform without added personality or persuasion.

Formal: use professional completeness and controlled politeness. Do not confuse formality with nominal style, ceremonial phrases, or long sentences.

Casual: allow contractions, fragments, humor, and uneven rhythm only when supported by the author and relationship.

Academic: preserve discipline conventions, citation scope, hedging, argument structure, and methodological caution.

Legal: apply only to legal or administrative contexts. Preserve operative terms, jurisdiction, effective date, rights, duties, and source hierarchy. Do not make unrelated writing sound like a lawyer.

Commercial: allow clear persuasion, benefit framing, proof, and calls to action. Remove unsupported hype, not persuasion itself.

## Punctuation and syntax

No punctuation mark is universal evidence of AI authorship. Follow the source style, house style, language, accessibility needs, and user preference. Simplify nested syntax when it blocks comprehension, but preserve necessary legal, technical, or academic relationships.

For localization or translation review, compare meaning, claim strength, authority, terminology, relationship, dates, numbers, and required institutional wording before optimizing fluency. Do not treat fluent target-language prose as sufficient evidence of an accurate localization.
