# Evidence and preservation

## Locks

Create a working ledger before any material rewrite.

Claim lock:

- numbers, percentages, money, units, dates, periods, and denominators
- names, organizations, products, job titles, qualifications, and responsibilities
- URLs, citations, DOI, ISBN, legal provisions, case numbers, and source scope
- quotations, code, filenames, field names, and technical terms
- uncertainty, attribution, causal strength, and authority level

Persona lock:

- first-person experience and ownership
- opinions, motivations, emotions, humor, and personal history
- pronouns, form of address, and relationship
- deliberate stylistic features supported by the user or samples

Structure lock when required:

- supplied template, rubric, section order, slide master, form fields, or machine-readable schema
- explicit cuts and text marked for retention

## Evidence states

Label material as one of:

- verified fact
- user-supplied fact
- source claim
- evidence-backed inference
- assumption
- unknown
- proposed wording or action

Do not turn a source claim into independent proof. Do not turn an assumption into customer, employer, or legal fact. Do not replace an unverifiable source with an invented citation.

## Revision comparison

Compare the final text with the original ledger, not only with the previous editing pass. Check claim-to-entity binding, qualifiers, units, currency, source attribution, causality, ownership, and intentional omissions.

Mechanical scripts can flag surface differences. They cannot certify semantic equivalence.
