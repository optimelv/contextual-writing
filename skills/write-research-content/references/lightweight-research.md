# Lightweight research

## Shared workflow

1. Convert the request into an answerable question or decision.
2. Define scope, exclusions, time boundary, geography, and evidence standard.
3. Build a source plan before searching broadly.
4. Verify sources and extract claim-level evidence.
5. Search for disagreement or disconfirming evidence.
6. Synthesize only what the evidence supports.
7. State limitations and next evidence needed.

## Source posture

Prefer sources that own or legally establish the fact for that question. Then use independent sources for corroboration, interpretation, user experience, or criticism. A company filing is authoritative for what the issuer disclosed, not independent proof that every statement is true. A community post can reveal real-world friction, not population-level prevalence.

Never fabricate citations, quotations, authors, dates, links, page numbers, cases, legal sections, statistics, or source contents. An unverifiable source is excluded or clearly marked open.

## Academic-light mode

Require a focused research question, field, intended output, date range, and source standard. Use peer-reviewed and authoritative literature appropriate to the field. Preserve hedging, distinguish methods and study designs, and report disagreement. Do not impose one evidence hierarchy across all disciplines.

Escalate to the Academic Research Suite for literature reviews, systematic search, methodology design, meta-analysis, paper pipelines, or formal citation-integrity gates.

## Legal-light mode

Require jurisdiction and effective date. Separate constitution or treaty, statute or regulation, binding case law, administrative guidance, official explanation, commentary, and proposed reform according to the actual legal system. Verify currentness and the exact proposition supported.

Do not generalize law across jurisdictions. Do not turn guidance into binding law or a proposed change into current law. Preserve legally operative wording. Recommend qualified review where rights, liability, deadlines, or material decisions are involved.

## Company mode

Clarify the decision: application, outreach, investment context, partnership, meeting preparation, competitor comparison, or general profile. Use the job description and official company sources for stated priorities and claims. Use filings and regulatory sources where applicable. Use reputable independent coverage for external context and criticism. Date every time-sensitive fact.

For application research, extract only evidence that can support a genuine, role-relevant motivation. Do not manufacture cultural fit or repeat marketing slogans.

## Product or market mode

Establish user, use case, location, budget, constraints, decision criteria, current availability, and date. Separate specifications, test data, expert evaluation, commercial claims, and anecdotal experience. Explain whether evidence transfers to the actual use case.

## Fact-check mode

Break the text into material claims. Return `supported`, `partly supported`, `unsupported`, `contradicted`, `outdated`, or `not verifiable`, with source and reasoning. Do not silently rewrite an unsupported claim into a stronger-sounding one.
