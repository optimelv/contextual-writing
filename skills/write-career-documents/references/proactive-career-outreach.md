# Proactive career outreach

Use this mode when a candidate approaches a founder, startup, investor, accelerator, hiring manager, or small team about a role, internship, project, or exploratory career conversation. The applicant is the subject of the application; this is not B2B sales outreach.

## Distinguish the outreach mode

- **Exploratory cold outreach:** no advertised role or formal application process; the immediate goal is a short, relevant conversation.
- **Speculative application:** no advertised role, but the candidate is explicitly proposing a role, internship, or contribution and may need to provide application documents.
- **Formal or referred application email:** a role, process, request, or referral already exists; follow its stated document and channel requirements.

Do not force the same structure, call to action, or attachment rule across these modes.
If the user's wording is compatible with more than one mode and the distinction changes the message or attachment decision, keep the mode open and ask for it. Do not label an exploratory inquiry as a speculative application, or vice versa, before the user resolves that distinction.

## Evidence before personalization

Resolve from existing context:

- recipient and why that person is relevant;
- target work, contribution area, timing, and location constraints;
- one current, attributable observation about the organization, team, portfolio, product, thesis, operating model, or role;
- source and date for volatile claims such as funding, valuation, portfolio relationships, hiring, launches, or performance;
- one or two verified applicant experiences that connect to the recipient's work;
- desired next step and channel constraints.

Use research content when current external facts are needed. Separate the sourced observation from the candidate's interpretation or fit hypothesis. Do not turn a public fact into evidence of private strategy, culture, causality, or hiring demand.

## Message construction

### Subject

Make the purpose and relevant connection recognizable. A named institution, shared context, role, function, or specific topic may help when it is truthful and useful to the recipient. Do not describe an institution as prestigious or use status as a substitute for relevance. Avoid clickbait, false familiarity, and generic subjects such as `Quick question` or `Interested in working at [Company]` when a more specific subject is available.

### Opening observation

Lead with a concise, verified observation when it creates a real reason for contacting this recipient. Add either:

- an authentic question the recipient is reasonably positioned to answer; or
- a restrained implication that explains why the observation matters to the candidate's intended work.

The question must arise from the observation rather than operate as a personalization trick. Avoid reciting news without a point, asking for confidential information, implying causality from portfolio outcomes, or asking something already answered by the supplied source.

### Specific fit and relevance proof

Name the organization-specific trait, work, or problem that actually matters. Then select one or two verified experiences, achievements, or operating exposures that support a plausible contribution. Connect them; do not paste a compressed CV, list every credential, invent passion, or claim cultural fit from public marketing.

Preserve exact ownership and scope. A candidate who supported modelling, research, operations, or fundraising must not be upgraded to having led the work. Treat grades, awards, university, and founder experience as optional evidence only when they strengthen the actual relevance argument.

### Call to action

Ask for one proportionate next step with a purpose. A 15-minute exploratory call can be appropriate, but it is not mandatory. Name what the conversation would clarify, for example whether the contribution area is relevant or who owns the topic. Do not manufacture urgency, demand a referral, or present a menu of competing asks.

## CV, links, and attachments

Make an explicit mode- and channel-based decision:

- For exploratory cold outreach, a concise message plus one current LinkedIn, portfolio, or profile link may be enough; offer the CV when useful.
- For a speculative application, attach the CV when target-market convention, recipient expectations, or the proposed role make evidence necessary; otherwise offer it or link a current profile.
- For a formal or referred application, follow the stated requirements and attach the requested documents.

Never claim that an attachment will or will not trigger spam without current provider-specific evidence. Avoid unexplained files, excessive links, tracking links, or a missing CV when the process explicitly requests one.

## Quality check

Before returning the draft, verify that:

- the company-specific sentences are sourced facts or clearly framed interpretations;
- the message could not be sent unchanged to a different organization;
- the applicant evidence is selected for the recipient's work, not prestige signalling;
- the question is genuine and answerable;
- there is one clear next action;
- the subject, length, link, and attachment decision fit the outreach mode.
