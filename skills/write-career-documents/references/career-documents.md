# Career documents

## Context first

Determine the market and document convention before enforcing or promising page count, photograph use, personal data, date format, spelling variant, or section order. A one-page resume is common in some early-career contexts, not a universal rule. Academic CVs, experienced profiles, public-sector applications, and country-specific formats differ. A broad sector label such as `consulting` is not enough to settle the format.

## Evidence ledger

For every role, project, or qualification capture:

- exact organization, title, location, and dates
- actual responsibility and ownership level
- output or action
- scope, method, stakeholder, or tool when relevant
- defensible result or decision relevance
- evidence source and uncertainty

Use the strongest verb supported by ownership. Distinguish `supported`, `contributed`, `prepared`, `managed`, and `led`. Do not upgrade participation into ownership or estimate attractive metrics.

## CV and resume

- order information by relevance and target convention
- use reverse chronology unless the context supports another structure
- make bullets easy to scan and specific enough to defend in an interview
- lead with contribution or outcome when supported; include tools only when they add meaning
- quantify when a reliable number improves interpretation, not as decoration
- avoid duties that add no differentiation and keyword stuffing that damages readability
- preserve exact credentials, protected titles, and official organization names
- optimize for the actual application system and human reviewer; do not claim a universal ATS formula
- inspect the exported file for hierarchy, page spill, line breaks, links, symbols, and selectable text
- fit the document to its actual content instead of enforcing one static combination of font size, spacing, and margins
- standardize date ranges across the document; when the template uses compact month and year dates, write `MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY` with spaces around an en dash, append an explicit uncertainty suffix for expected completion dates, and use one locale-appropriate current-state label such as `heute` or `present`

Useful bullet structure:

`Accurate action verb + work or output + relevant scope or method + defensible result or purpose`

## Cover letter

Build one coherent argument rather than repeating the CV:

1. role and organization-specific reason for applying
2. relevant capability supported by selected evidence
3. credible connection between the need and the applicant's contribution
4. concise close appropriate to the market

Research the organization and role before claiming fit. Distinguish official company claims, role requirements, independent evidence, and inference. A company- or role-motivation sentence that would remain equally valid after replacing the organization or role must be made specific, supported, or removed. Evidence paragraphs may transfer between applications when the underlying experience is true, but select and connect them for the actual role rather than repeating the CV indiscriminately.

Make the target-specific reasoning do real work: connect one verified organization, role, team, programme, event, product, thesis, or operating-model detail to an applicant experience or intended contribution. Do not merely repeat the target's marketing language. If the brief lacks enough evidence for a selective target, leave the draft provisional or request research rather than filling the gap with admiration.

End with the one action or closing convention the process requires. Avoid a final list of generic virtues such as analytical, collaborative, entrepreneurial, or impact-driven when the body already needs to demonstrate them. A concise direct close is stronger than an unsupported summary of fit.

Do not invent passion, lifelong motivation, personal history, cultural fit, or causal stories between unrelated experiences.

Do not infer retrospective learning, capability development, or personal growth from an activity. Phrases such as `taught me`, `showed me`, `confirmed my interest`, `made me realise`, `strengthened my ability`, `equipped me`, or `allowed me to develop` are first-person experience claims and require supplied evidence or explicit user authorization. Describe the verified work and its relevance directly when the learning or development claim was not provided.

## Native document templates

For an editable Word deliverable, use a user-supplied native template first. If none exists and the market and use case fit, choose the native asset by evidence density and document type:

- `assets/generic-cv-template.docx` for a dense consulting-style CV with substantial verified evidence
- `assets/generic-cv-template-early-career.docx` for a student or recent graduate with limited verified experience
- `assets/generic-cover-letter-template.docx` for a formal cover letter

Treat all fictional template content as replaceable slots, never as applicant evidence.

Preserve the template's visual system while adapting it to the amount of verified content. Follow [adaptive document layout](adaptive-document-layout.md). When native authoring is available, do not ship chat-only Markdown when the user requested a formatted file. When it is unavailable, provide complete structured content and disclose the missing file and render capability instead of pretending that a native artifact exists.

## Short fields and profiles

Answer the exact prompt directly. Respect character and word limits, recount after the final edit, and keep drafting notes outside the submitted field. Use first person only when the format supports it. A professional biography may require third person, but must not introduce unsupported prestige or credentials.

## Review

Check every claim for evidence, ownership, relevance, consistency, audience fit, and interview defensibility. Treat spelling, grammar, and formatting as necessary but not sufficient.
